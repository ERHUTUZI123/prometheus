## Exact six-action rival

The hidden family in `test-contract.md` has a movable local trace x and one modulated plasticity process

    Y(b): (y,z,w) <- (y,z,w) + b*(1,f(x),g(x)),

where f is affine and g is quadratic with a nonzero quadratic coefficient. These facts describe the evaluation family; the learner need not receive its coefficients or a desired word. A standard response-identification controller can probe Y at three trace values x0,x0+h,x0+2h, fit the three actual response columns v_j=(1,f(x_j),g(x_j)), and solve

    [v_0 v_1 v_2] b = (0,0,r).

It then uses the six physical actions

    Y(b0); X(h); Y(b1); X(h); Y(b2); X(-2h).                 (1)

All x,y,z are restored, and w changes by r. In the canonical family f(x)=x, g(x)=x^2/2, one solution is

    (b0,b1,b2) = (r/h^2)*(1,-2,1).

This is an ordinary finite-difference/nullspace steering construction. It uses measured columns and a small linear solve; it is not supplied the abc law or the nested commutator word. Each pulse's amplitude bounds must be checked; for comparable |r| and h the three-pulse method can fit the same bounds, while a wider range requires more pulses or a different h and must be paid. The nested R word costs ten primitive actions. Thus the clean worked family does not support a runtime superiority claim for that word.

There is also a fair acquisition route: at three trace settings, probe one signed modulation and measure the actual forward-output response; fit low-degree response functions or retain the measured response columns. With access to trace/context and output probes identical to R, this is an inexpensive standard identification problem. Include negative-amplitude and held-out-state checks because finite data alone do not establish a global affine response. R has the same finite-scope difficulty.

## What this does and does not decide

The rival itself acquires a reusable update operation and a demand-to-argument law. Across future targets, the solved amplitudes scale with r, and the trace returns to its previous value. It therefore enters on the central acquisition/retention outcome, not just on pointwise function matching. Its calibration, inverse/staging assumptions, solve work, stored columns and all six actions are charged.

This refutes any interpretation in which the proposed polynomial example already establishes a new efficient organization beyond known response identification/control. A random-word CEM failure would be particularly misleading here. The primary rival should include this identified-response/nullspace controller, not rely only on generic shooting. Its strong assumption is a recurring single-trace additive plasticity family with a small response span; that assumption is genuinely satisfied by the proposed audit, rather than granted as an oracle only to the rival.

It does not refute the broader possibility of reusable learned cancellation programs in nonlinear plastic substrates where actions alter one another's response fields, many local contexts interact, and full identification is expensive. That broader possibility now needs a concrete family and a mechanism-specific reason a small acquired program is cheaper than targeted response identification or parameterized options. Replacing the polynomial toy by arbitrary black-box dynamics without such a reason would not repair the scientific gap.

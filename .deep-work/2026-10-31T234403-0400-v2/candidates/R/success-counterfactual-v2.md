# R v2 success counterfactual

**Continue R; change its causal mechanism.** V1 learned a data structure's operational presentation, but did not explain why the presentation should improve learning. V2 asks whether a network can construct a computational resource *together with a compact, compositionally closed space of useful edits to that resource*. The same acquired multiplication rules then transport both execution and credit through future programs. A credit component outside that closed space becomes an inspectable request to construct another channel.

The important limitation is the tradeoff between long-lived, reusable computation and affordable exact adaptation. Generic recurrent credit can require retaining a trajectory or a large state–parameter sensitivity array. Fixed cheap recurrent architectures avoid this by designer-selected structure; approximate online methods trade exactness for cost. A learned resource that remains closed under its own useful edits could carry exact sensitivities through arbitrarily long finite compositions using a reusable small carrier. The prospect is learning an organization that makes subsequent learning tractable, not claiming that a better normal form changes mathematical derivatives.

Full success would matter beyond a small matrix example if a network could acquire such carriers for recurring families of variable-length operator programs, grow them when new interactions become useful, and amortize the discovery/compilation cost across subsequent tasks. Weighted transducers, recurrent memory operators and modular dynamical computations are an initial broad class. The matrix realization below makes closure and credit inspectable; it is a foothold, not a claim that all neural computation is linear.

The potential change in organization is: **a retained mechanism contains its own reusable representation of how it can be changed.** Composition of mechanisms composes their edit transport, rather than asking a generic global learner to reconstruct all credit from the whole trace. A newly useful interaction may force both a new execution channel and new transported credit channels. This is the v2 hypothesis, not an established novel result.

Actual strong rivals are exact forward-mode/RTRL with sparse structure exploited, UORO's rank-one unbiased sensitivity estimate, dynamic sparse growth using task gradients, and fixed structured recurrent families. None should be weakened to a parameter-count-only baseline. A generic sparse forward-mode compiler may already recover all of the proposed closure advantage; that is a serious coverage possibility to test directly, not a reason to define every possible rival as the entire proposed loop.

The first finite check will examine the new interaction, not another memory-type demonstration: a task-gradient-selected edit E23 composed with an existing E12 requires a new E13 credit channel. A basis that retains only the initiating edit gives a wrong derivative; closure must recover the exact dense forward derivative. This can check the mechanism and its failure mode. It cannot establish neural acquisition, cheaper learning, or novelty.

Success remains conditional on a hard missing step: acquiring useful low-cost closure rather than either growing to the full derivative space or forbidding the variations that future tasks need. All discovery, dense-gradient probes, basis construction, closure checks and kernel costs must be counted. There is no universal compression claim.

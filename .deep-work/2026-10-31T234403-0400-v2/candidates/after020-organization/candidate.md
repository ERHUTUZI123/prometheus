## Important possible effect

A neural system may need a new kind of internal state, not just a new answer or a longer execution, to carry task-relevant history into later computation. The hypothesis is that actual experience can guide the formation of a persistent, controllable dynamical mode together with its learned write/clear/read conditions, and that this state can later participate in other computations. Success would make the acquisition of a new internal state machine component an explicit learning event, with its conditions retained and revisable, rather than assuming sufficient useful persistent variables and their boundaries are already supplied.

Known latches, gated RNNs/LSTM, recurrent network growth, finite-state identification, gradients and fixed numerical rules are legitimate constructive starting points and strong alternatives. Creating a bit or crossing a pitchfork is not itself a foundational contribution. The important prospect is a task-grounded formation signal and revisable learned interface that changes subsequent capability acquisition at honest costs. Novelty, superiority and economical acquisition are OPEN.

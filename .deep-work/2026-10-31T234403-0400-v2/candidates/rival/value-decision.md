# Rival value decision — acquisition is the surviving question

**Judgment: preserve both concrete acquisition hypotheses; do not escalate to a major training system from the present checks.** R's law-to-resource construction and K's exact execution/derivative machinery are coherent. Their hoped-for consequence is consequential: acquiring an organization could lower the cost of constructing further capabilities while retaining earlier ones. The checked literature already supplies much of the representational machinery. This is partial mechanism coverage, not proof that either complete candidate already exists. The decision therefore turns on an acquisition phenomenon, not a new name for an algebra or an exact identity test. This is no architecture vote.

**R has two importantly different readings.** v0 asks whether opaque action relations can be identified from replay. Such tests require an independent action system; evaluating a proposed equation inside the quotient runtime enforcing it is circular. The now-read v1 instead chooses internal operational laws, instantiates them, and selects useful presentations by task feedback. That is legitimate construction, not identification of a hidden true algebra. The v0 objection does not refute v1.

**Closest checked R boundary.** [Guttag 1977](https://cecs.wright.edu/~tkprasad/courses/cs7100/guttag-cacm77.pdf), §§2–4, already defines data resources through operational relationships and refines their implementations. [Phillips 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7864426/), Results and Methods, reconstructs a common monoid and applies free-M-set completion for transfer. [QuickSpec](https://smallbone.se/papers/quickspec.pdf), §2, discovers equations by testing terms and pruning their consequences. [CALF 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2001.05786), §§2,5–6, learns algebras over a supplied functor; its quotient structure is part of that setup. [Sargsyan 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.16123v1), Construction 3.2, compiles supplied generators/relations into neural architecture. These do not establish task-selected acquisition of useful generative memory organizations. They do remove “laws define resources,” “relations can be learned,” and “algebra affords transfer” as sufficient differentiators.

**R's differentiating acquisition claim:** under one generic runtime and a relation grammar that does not encode a resource catalogue, task feedback selects reusable operational contracts, including held-out combinations of constraints, whose installation improves subsequent acquisition/retention at lower total cost than implementing the same effects through a growing program library. Count law search, task trials, normalization, allocation, retained histories, compiler construction and rollback. Strong controls: the same presentation language/runtime with an enumerative or MDL search; a neural program-library learner with the same allocation, observation and feedback primitives; and a supplied-resource upper bound. A small stack demonstration cannot settle this claim. R v1 now directly executes the rules as programs and names the same-grammar CEGIS control. Its remaining distinct acquisition effect is undefined, so the implementation case is incomplete; this is not a proof of full prior-work equivalence.

**K's repaired local derivative claim survives.** Multilinearity gives each ambient semantic derivative as a contraction with one tensor replaced by its local derivative. With k existing degree-d exceptions, adding one derivative insertion costs at most poly(N,bitlength)·2^{d(k+1)} per insertion under the stipulated pinned-core evaluator. This is a valid way to leave a compiled core. It is not a new differentiation principle. [Liao et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.09650), §II, covers tensor-network AD. [Mocherla et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.02654), §§1.5–4, treats matchgates plus explicit non-Gaussian departures; [Mele–Herasymenko](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.18665), Theorem 26 and Algorithm 1, constructively localizes non-Gaussianity and learns a compressing Gaussian frame. Their state/unitary and measurement assumptions differ from K's tensor task-learning problem.

**K's differentiating acquisition claim:** useful semantic learning repeatedly spends a bounded exception budget, then exact recompilation releases that budget without discarding acquired behavior, enabling later capabilities/compositions that the best matched fixed-core learner plus deterministic compiler does not acquire at the same total cost. Give that rival the same full local gradients, sparse updates, exact signatures, allowed region moves, recognizers and retained library. K's post-review quartic conjugation gives a genuine exact release family: A(cI+i sQ)A†=cI−sPQ for anticommuting quartics and A=(I+iP)/√2; PQ is quadratic. The same deterministic compiler can exploit it. What remains missing is acquisition of useful release paths beyond such supplied identities.

**P remains a genuinely different boundary:** it extracts intervention-stable state interfaces from executed neural computation rather than selecting an operational presentation (R) or re-expressing a fixed tensor semantics (K). Its all-state solution, chart cost and later-learning benefit remain separate open questions. Neither R nor K's source coverage decides P.

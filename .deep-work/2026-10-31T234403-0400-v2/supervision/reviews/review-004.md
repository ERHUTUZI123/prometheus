## Explicit acquisition rule

**AGENT PROPOSAL / RECONSTRUCTION.** An owner maintains a bounded set of already available call-context features c, its actual nonlinear implementation, and a small routing program `pi(c)`. Features may describe internal dataflow/provenance accessible at execution. They must not be latent desired-group labels, future task answers, or a lookup table of evaluation call IDs. New owners retain their actual parameters and any later acquired repair procedures; nothing is reconstructed from deleted state.

At an actual failure to find a satisfactory shared repair within the current attempt budget:

1. **Collect owned repair evidence at a common base.** Record the affected call contexts, available current-task targets and retained-output constraints, actual edit proposals and executed consequences. Local AD gradients may be used as a designed proposal signal; using them does not disqualify the mechanism. For protected behavior already at its optimum, zero loss gradient does not mean that arbitrary edits are harmless: retain output constraints or actual replay evidence.
2. **Propose a small context split.** Enumerate a predeclared, bounded family of simple routing predicates, such as a depth-one or depth-two tree over available features. Rank splits by disagreement of the requested call edits. A gradient version has the precise normalized proxy below. The acquired object is the selected executable predicate, not a human-supplied partition.
3. **Compare real finite repairs before retaining a split.** Replay from the same saved base under (a) the best shared finite-repair candidates available within the comparison budget, (b) the proposed ownership split and its repairs, and (c) actual retained-output checks. Shared candidates must include the finite/nonlocal repair mechanisms already available to the learner; do not restrict the baseline to the local tangent or one gradient step. Charge failed attempts, copies, routing, additional owned parameters and subsequent execution. Failed bounded shared search is evidence at that budget, never a proof that a shared solution is impossible.
4. **Retain only a justified program.** Accept a split only if its replayed task/retention improvement justifies its stated added cost under the frozen criterion. Otherwise keep sharing. Preserve the resulting predicate and owned state as part of the routine's internal learning interface. Reuse it at new call compositions through their available context features, rather than relearning an N-by-N tie mask for each program.

This is a specific rule for acquiring a change in update organization. Its current uncertainty is whether compact context predicates and finite repair experience capture reusable controllability structure rather than one program's incidental gradients. It does not yet answer how every nonlinear repair program is acquired, and should not be represented as doing so.

### A checkable proposal statistic and its limits

Let g_i be call-coordinate gradients at one declared base. To avoid mistaking owner multiplicity for a larger effective step size, first compare normalized owner averaging:

`Pi_P = S(S^T S)^(-1)S^T`,

`delta_theta = -eta (Pi_P tensor I_p) g`.

For a partition P, the squared projected gradient is

`A(P)=sum_(B in P) |B| ||mean_(i in B) g_i||^2`.

Splitting B into B1,B2 changes it by exactly

`A(P_split)-A(P)=|B1||B2|/|B| * ||mean(g_B1)-mean(g_B2)||^2`.

This follows by expanding the weighted within/between-mean identity. It is a cheap split-proposal statistic, not a new optimization theorem, a certificate of incompatibility, or evidence of future usefulness. With a fixed call-space edit-norm cap, use the corresponding normalized descent proposal and account for the actual finite edit cost; the squared-gradient statistic only ranks first-order opportunities. If ordinary unnormalized owner SGD is used instead, its block-size-dependent mobility must be matched explicitly in the comparison.

**Mandatory nonlinear attack:** as the coordinator derived, at `theta=-1`, retaining `theta^2=1` while acquiring `theta=1` has a shared finite solution `theta -> +1` despite a zero retention tangent. At `theta=-0.1`, `(theta^2-1)^2` and `(theta-1)^2` have opposing gradients but share the zero-loss solution `theta=1`. Therefore no gradient-conflict threshold may declare fission necessary. A common finite repair must be allowed to beat the split. If a strict finite edit-radius or retention-at-every-intermediate-state constraint makes the common jump unavailable, state that restriction and report only the resulting finite-budget claim.

## Success counterfactual and significance risk

If successful, a reusable nonlinear computation would acquire a compact description of **which uses can safely share future changes**, and carry that description into new compositions. That could make internal reuse and later specialization compatible without hand-assigning parameter ownership for every assembled program. The relevant potential change is in acquisition and composition of editable mechanisms, not increased universal computability or more neurons.

The strongest rival explanation is ordinary conditional parameter sharing/architecture search with a good heuristic. That may explain the whole result. The test should expose this possibility early without vetoing a constructive pass solely because the mechanism uses known copying, AD, clustering or kernels. A positive toy result is an intermediate causal result; scientific significance would still require showing that the acquired ownership abstraction remains compact and useful beyond the designed case.

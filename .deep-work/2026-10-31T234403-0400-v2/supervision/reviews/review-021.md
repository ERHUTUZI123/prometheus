## Mathematical assessment of the first diagnostic

For balanced histories with complete causal-state distributions P+ and P−, the optimal unrestricted state-readout error is `(1−TV(P+,P−))/2`. The pointwise minimum-probability derivation checks. This diagnoses information available at the stated checkpoint; it is not a free readout module, a trained probe's guaranteed accuracy or a lower bound on every future architecture.

For `z'=tanh(kappa z)`, kappa>1, nonzero fixed points ±a retain separation 2a during a noiseless hold. At either point, `r=kappa(1−a²)` lies strictly between zero and one: substituting `kappa=atanh(a)/a` and integrating `1/(1−u²)` gives the strict upper bound. The tangent after D steps is r^D. The fading map `z'=rz` has that identical tangent but separation `2a r^D`. Thus vanishing within-basin gradients do not establish missing categorical memory or require a unit eigenvalue for persistence. The claim that they do is refuted; the persistent-mode hypothesis is not.

With Gaussian noise of standard deviation sigma applied **only at the query cut**, the optimal balanced errors are `Phi(−a/sigma)` and `Phi(−a r^D/sigma)`. These formulas check. They do not describe per-step dynamical noise, where basin escapes and finite-horizon mixing require separate analysis.

Query-cut rounding and rounding after each update must remain separate. In particular, nearest-grid per-step rounding can make a fading map stall at a nonzero k·delta when `|(1−r)k|≤1/2`, creating an artificial persistent state. A diagnostic should label that effect rather than attribute it to the unrounded dynamics. This is a useful condition to inspect in the proposed small process, not a fatal flaw in the assay.

The cue-cut derivative is not the full shared-parameter gradient. Later parameter uses contribute additional paths. Rare demanded queries can also multiply the unconditional signal by their frequency without any temporal attenuation. The audit handles both distinctions correctly. Mean distance alone is insufficient for general distributions; a finite probe's failure does not certify equality of full-state distributions.

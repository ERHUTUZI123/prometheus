\section{Monodromy Learning}
\label{sec:monodromy-learning-main}

The learning mechanism distinguishes changing an executable operation from
adapting its realization. A concrete supplied covering is
\begin{equation}
 F_D(z,u)=z^D-Dz-u=0,\qquad
 \mathcal C_D=\{-(D-1)\zeta:\zeta^{D-1}=1\},
 \label{eq:main-polynomial-cover}
\end{equation}
over controls outside $\mathcal C_D$. At basepoint $u_*=0$, the roots are
marked once. A neural controller emits polygon vertices with fixed endpoints;
continuation determines the resulting sheet action. The tested component uses
the cubic instance, while the general interface requires an appropriate
covering and a computable execution domain.

Raw coordinates $a_\theta$ determine the loop class. An unadmitted loop may
change class between two regular parameter snapshots; the interpolating family
can meet the discriminant, but that singular interpolation is not executed.
The safe-realization interface, developed in earlier constructions but not
jointly validated as a separate parameter layer in the present cubic hybrid,
uses an admissible deformation parameter $\omega$ to move a realization
through a nonsingular based homotopy. Such same-class adaptation can improve
execution quality while preserving its operation. It cannot supply the
class-changing learning signal. A changed loop counts as a new operation only
when its endpoint action changes.

\subsection{Coupled gradients from terminal feedback}

Exact endpoint-semantic losses are locally constant in regular loop
coordinates. For forming operations, introduce a normalized product of smooth
location kernels with bounded support and optimize
\begin{equation}
 J_\sigma(\theta)=\int L(v;\theta)p_\sigma(v-a_\theta)\,dv.
\end{equation}
The parameter affects both the sampling distribution and the neural
computation inside the loss. Its derivative is
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \nabla_\theta J_\sigma&=
 \mathbb E[\partial_\theta L(V;\theta)\vert_{V\,\mathrm{fixed}}]
 +(D_\theta a_\theta)^\top g,\\
 g_j&=\mathbb E_{V_{-j}}\!\left[
       \sum_r\Delta L_r\,p_\sigma(b_r-a_{\theta,j})\right].
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:main-coupled-gradient}
\end{equation}
Here $b_r$ are discriminant-crossing events along a coordinate slice, and
$\Delta L_r$ is the terminal-loss jump between adjacent chambers. The first
term backpropagates through encoding, calling, readout, and their shared
backbone with executed maps fixed. The second chains measured class feedback
through the same controller. This combines established stochastic-gradient
calculus with boundary differentiation~\cite{schulman2015stochastic,lee2018reparameterization}.

For a free polygon-vertex coordinate, only two adjacent segments move. Each
segment supplies at most one generic intersection with each critical control.
Event locations can therefore be found without enumerating complete operator
classes. Safe chamber representatives supply actual terminal losses; their
execution, sorting, continuation, and observations are counted. Sampling means
remain inside the domain eroded by the kernel support. Replacing a numerically
difficult sample by a certified representative of its class preserves the
ideal semantic objective rather than silently conditioning its distribution.

\subsection{Protected update transactions}

For an admitted loop, a maximum vertex displacement smaller than its old
discriminant clearance certifies a based homotopy. Each proposed shared update
also checks the numerical execution domain, old native binding closure, and
the soft/native path-mass condition of
Section~\ref{sec:theory-main}. These obligations protect different interfaces.

The implemented transaction first backtracks a joint parameter proposal.
When every positive joint scale is blocked, an optional fallback retries the
same gradient restricted to existing encoder, readout, and call-head weights,
holding the shared backbone and loop head fixed. All certificates still apply;
only one proposal is committed. This block-coordinate feasibility repair
preserves usable calling credit without changing the learning signal or adding
parameters. It is an optimization mechanism rather than a new topological
primitive. Its trials, masked components, and maintenance costs remain explicit.

## Du, Li and Mordatch (2020), Compositional Visual Generation with Energy Based Models

[Original NeurIPS paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2020/file/49856ed476ad01fcff881d57e161d73f-Paper.pdf). Read Section 3.1-3.2, printed pp. 3-4 (PDF indices 2-3), equations (1)-(5), and introduction pp. 1-2. Their variables are image x, concept code c_i, neural energy E_theta(x|c_i). They learn distributions by contrastive updates and use Langevin state refinement. Conjunction is a product of distributions, equivalently summation of their energies; generation descends the sum with noise. This directly covers energy-addition composition. Concepts are separately specified/trained here; it does not by itself establish discovery of latent factorization or internal algorithm formation. Our inference: simply composing learned potentials is already an explicit mechanism, not an unoccupied foundational gap.

## Du et al. (2021), Unsupervised Learning of Compositional Energy Concepts (COMET)

[Original paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.03042). Read Section 3.1-3.2, printed pp. 3-4 (PDF indices 2-3), equations (1)-(2), Algorithm 1; Section 4's energy-versus-mask comparison on p. 5. Their encoder Enc_theta(x_i) infers component codes z_k. A shared energy E_theta(x;z_k) represents each component; x is reconstructed by minimizing the sum of those energies. Finite gradient steps approximate the minimizer and the reconstruction objective trains the system. This covers neural factor formation by reconstruction, descent execution, and subsequent component composition more directly than v0 acknowledges. Differences: our proposed masks address state-coordinate support, and our desired relations are computational; these differences alone establish no new principle or resource advantage. The paper's comparison to a particular masking decoder is not a theorem against arbitrary recurrent or factor-graph rivals.

## Du et al. (2022), Learning Iterative Reasoning through Energy Minimization (IREM)

[Original ICML/PMLR paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/du22d/du22d.pdf). Read Section 3.1-3.2, printed p. 3 (PDF index 2), equations (2)-(6), and Algorithm 1 on p. 4. Variables x and y denote problem and candidate solution; y advances by negative energy gradient. Learning regresses the finite-step answer against the supervised solution by differentiating through optimization. The authors explicitly note that training at finite steps need not produce a consistent longer-run landscape. Our inference: the core of v0's end-task formation rule is directly covered. Read the limitations on printed p. 9: their discrete-output and training-cost difficulties are relevant cautions, not a universal no-go theorem for all dissipative computing. No claims about their external-memory extension are needed or investigated here.

## Scellier and Bengio (2017), Equilibrium Propagation

[Original paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.05179). Read Section 2.1-2.2, printed pp. 2-4, equations (1)-(5), and the first-page mechanism overview. They distinguish neural states u/s, weights W and biases b, internal energy E, target cost C, and total energy F=E+beta C. With inputs clamped, state dynamics follow -partial_s F and decrease F; training weakly nudges output states rather than requiring a wholly distinct neuronal executor. This is a checked precedent for state dissipation with externally optimized parameters. Our v0 uses ordinary finite-step differentiation instead; neither state descent nor task-driven energy learning is claimed new. I did not read the complete general theorem/proof and do not invoke its correctness beyond these stated dynamics.

## Bessiere et al. (2013), Constraint Acquisition via Partial Queries (QUACQ)

[Original IJCAI paper](https://www.lirmm.fr/constraintacquisition/docs/papers/ijcai13-quacq.pdf). Read Section 2-3.3, PDF indices 1-3, including Algorithms 1-3 and Proposition 2/Theorem 1. Variables are vocabulary (X,D), target constraint network C_T, constraint bias B, current learned network C_L. Rejected examples cause FindScope and FindC to add a constraint; positive answers eliminate incompatible bias constraints. A partial query is accepted when no fully scoped target constraint rejects it, which differs from existence of a global completion. Their bounds assume the stated bias/language and partial-query semantics. Our repair's extension test instead derives sound no-goods using complete validity queries, potentially exponentially many. It cannot inherit QUACQ's query bounds. This is a concrete strong precedent for event-based constraint creation, and a matched symbolic constraint learner remains a primary rival.

## New primary-source entry: invertible learning, exact locations

**SOURCE — Dinh, Sohl-Dickstein and Bengio, Density estimation using Real NVP, arXiv:1605.08803v3.** Entered the original HTML body at [§§3.2–3.5, Eq.4–5 and 8–13](https://arxiv.org/html/1605.08803), not merely the abstract. The affine coupling leaves one coordinate block unchanged and applies a learned conditional scale/translation to the other. Its inverse subtracts the translation and divides by the scale; it does not invert the networks computing those functions. Alternating such couplings gives an invertible composition. Our additive shear is the unit-scale special case. This source establishes an existing primitive; it does not by these passages establish the proposed anchored old-operation retention application.

**SOURCE — Behrmann et al., Invertible Residual Networks, ICML 2019.** Entered [original PDF pp.2–3, §2, Theorem1, Algorithm1, Eq.1, Lemma2 and §2.1](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/behrmann19a/behrmann19a.pdf). A residual block I+g is invertible when Lip(g)<1; inversion uses a fixed-point iteration with a geometric error bound, and forward/inverse Lipschitz bounds are explicit. The discussion distinguishes practical power-iteration estimates from a true spectral-norm guarantee. This supports an alternative invertible parameter family and the need to count inverse/certification work. The current construction instead uses an analytic shear inverse and needs no residual-network spectral test.

The original papers' experimental sections were not audited. No empirical claim here relies on them.

# D: causal attack and surviving question

The constructive phase produced an actual Get/Put/Valid machine and a computable learning law. The attack does **not** show that neural systems cannot construct editable representations. It shows that the current mechanism core is mostly existing bidirectional programming plus library learning, and the implemented probe is far smaller than the unresolved claim.

## ATTACK 1: substantive prior equivalence

The equations defining v0's view, round trips and composition coincide with lenses (sources/D/prior-attack.md). Optician already synthesizes such transformations from supplied formats and examples. DreamCoder already learns executable library abstractions and a neural search policy together. A rename or a composition of these systems would not itself establish a new principle. This is a substantive equivalence for the proposed core, not the vacuous argument that a universal computer can emulate it.

The surviving candidate is narrower and still bold: infer **the target editable schema, its valid intervention interface, and its raw implementation jointly from downstream raw tasks**, rather than infer a transformation between schemas supplied by the researcher. That full construction is unbuilt. This source comparison leaves its novelty OPEN, not established.

## ATTACK 2: the representational type can carry the answer

The executed probe provides a fold whose records already contain a symbol and a length, a decoder loop that repeats that symbol, and a field-program family `a*n+b`. It synthesizes boundary logic and small expressions, but does not construct the object schema, fold state, decoder control flow or field-access vocabulary. The most significant information needed for the worked example is therefore already in its scaffold. Correct execution cannot repair this inferential gap.

The training I/O is also a hand-designed family. The labels `increment` and `double` have no role in candidate execution or scoring, but the examples themselves select multiplicity transformations. No claim that the example's group/count semantics arose from natural raw experience follows.

## ATTACK 3: valid edits can change identity

View `[(a,1),(b,1)]` changed to `[(a,1),(a,1)]` decodes to `aa`, whose canonical parse is `[(a,2)]`. The edited objects have merged. PutGet fails if the requested two-object identity is promised. This is an executed counterexample, not merely a concern. The interface must reject the edit, preserve a boundary complement, or explicitly change object identity and recheck callers. Adding a label `object` does not resolve the semantics.

Similarly, grouping all occurrences by symbol loses spatial order: `aba` becomes `[(a,2),(b,1)]` and decodes to `aab`. A useful observational statistic does not automatically carry useful writeback.

## ATTACK 4: round trips do not establish task value or identifiability

Identity is a perfect editable representation. Any invertible relabeling phi yields an equivalent representation, with operations conjugated by phi; the same task outputs can be recovered. Round-trip laws cannot identify a privileged abstraction. Their role is interface coherence. The scientific comparison must concern finite-budget construction and execution on genuinely later tasks, modulo explicitly stated functional equivalence.

Finite law tests are incomplete. For any finite challenge set, a general program can agree there and fail elsewhere. A candidate-defined Valid predicate can also trivially reject inconvenient states. Consequently, observed no-op round trips and a few successful edits must not be called a universally certified new type. Validity coverage must itself be evaluated; a typed lens calculus can guarantee some laws by construction, but supplying that calculus is another explicit inductive bias.

## ATTACK 5: cost debt and conservative compilation

The per-operation v0 implementation reparses a raw input, changes record fields and decodes. Explicit fused raw algorithms solve these two example tasks with fewer counted operations. The prototype also eagerly evaluates its four candidate atoms, so its overhead is partly an implementation artifact. Neither its instruction ratio nor its success rate estimates a neural discovery advantage. No matched search baseline was executed.

The full learner's construction cost can be very large. If at most B^N ASTs represent each of Get, Put and Valid, naive triple enumeration has a loose B^(3N) candidate bound before task-program search. With C raw law cases, E edited-view cases and fuel F per execution, even checking one candidate costs O((C+E)F), plus synthesis/execution of each task program. A neural proposal can reorder this search but cannot make its cost disappear. Full cognitive/discovery costs, including the language model's work and human grammar design, were not measured.

The fixed interpreter's global reachable behaviors do not expand when views are admitted; what could change is finite-budget accessibility and compositional bookkeeping. That possible advantage remains a HYPOTHESIS.

## REVISE, not an automatic second experiment

The exact algebraic correction is to require GetPut and PutGet on a clearly specified domain; PutPut is an optional stronger promise, not a universal requirement. Foster's source makes this explicit. In the simple lossless decoder probe PutPut is structural and therefore not independent empirical evidence.

A future realization should keep a constructed view resident during internal computation, charge raw materialization at external boundaries, and version/invalidate cached views after raw edits. This can avoid repeated parse/decode overhead but is ordinary compilation/caching; it is not by itself a new architecture contribution.

Decision: STOP this finite first stage. Keep the high-upside joint schema/interface discovery question OPEN, while closing any novelty claim for the v0 lens algebra and any claim that the small probe built the full learner. No additional experiment is authorized by this note.

## 1. Kirschner and Gerhart: regulation can change assembly without redesigning assembly

Source: *The Plausibility of Life*, local 330-page PDF. Book title/copyright edition is not yet bibliographically reconciled with the library filename's 2008 label; do not infer original publication year from the filename. Selected complete pages extracted: printed 109-118 / PDF 125-134; printed 143-152 / PDF 159-168; printed 171-176 / PDF 187-192; printed 219-222 / PDF 235-238.

### Weak linkage

SOURCE, printed pp. 110-112 and 115-118: regulatory linkage concerns how information passes between components. Their weak linkage is minimally demanding and redirectable; it is not simply a mechanically weak bond. In the repressor example, a small molecule affects a repressor, which affects transcription of an already competent downstream system. Relocating a suitable binding site can change what responds without separately redesigning the downstream machinery. The authors attribute the term to Michael Conrad (1990); that original text has not yet been read.

RECONSTRUCTION (schematic, not their equation): let A(s) be a regulatory state and P(x;A) a functioning process. A rewiring from A(s) to A(s') changes *when* P is invoked, while retaining the complex transition law inside P. This reduces the number of interfaces that must co-vary only if P already remains competent over the new invocation range. It does not explain the initial invention of P or its out-of-range behavior.

### Exploratory assembly and selective stabilization

SOURCE, printed pp. 149-152: microtubules grow and collapse; turnover is dynamic rather than a static spoke arrangement. A peripheral stabilizing activity can prevent tip depolymerization. Filaments encountering it persist, while others turn over. The peripheral placement of stabilization therefore changes the array generated by the same assembly machinery. The text explicitly notes that other forces also contribute to mitotic spindle construction. Do not claim pure random search is a complete explanation of spindle assembly.

RECONSTRUCTION (minimal stochastic law): each tentative object j has state z_j, lifetime hazard mu_0, and local capture variable c_j in {0,1}. Sample growth z'_j ~ K(z'_j|z_j). Set survival hazard mu_j = mu_0*(1-c_j)+mu_capture*c_j with mu_capture << mu_0. A change in the spatial field producing c_j changes the persistent population distribution without changing K. In a stationary birth-death approximation n(z) is proportional to birth_rate(z)/mu(z); a region with lower loss therefore accumulates persistent objects. This is an explanatory reconstruction, not a fitted biological model.

Load-bearing assumption: the stabilizing event correlates with useful organization. An arbitrary capture field merely stabilizes arbitrary structures. Random exploration must actually encounter useful regions within available time and energy. The book's broad language about possible forms does not supply a quantitative coverage theorem.

### Accommodation reduces simultaneous change requirements

SOURCE, printed pp. 171-176: the limb discussion describes adapting muscle precursors, nerves and vasculature to altered skeletal arrangements. Their proposed consequence is that not every supportive tissue needs an independently coordinated inherited alteration at each step. This is the source's explanatory argument, not a new causal experiment established by our reading.

RECONSTRUCTION: for primary structural parameter g and supporting state a, phenotype is F(g,a). If support adapts by a*(g)=Adapt(g), a single change Delta g can produce F(g+Delta g,a*(g+Delta g)) instead of the usually damaged F(g+Delta g,a*(g)). This gives a precise candidate mapping from variation to coherent phenotype. It need not improve future adaptation; it can erase effects, consume resources or fail outside its range.

SOURCE, printed p. 220: the book limits the theory to the variation component and separates that from selective conditions over a population. This directly prevents claiming that neural accommodation proves evolvability or open-endedness.

Reasoning change: initially I considered adding reusable circuits. Reading instead suggests storing *adaptive reconstructors* whose accepted outcomes remain functional as the surrounding context changes. Reuse without accommodation misses the simultaneous-coordination gap.

## 2. Alon: the regulated observable and feedback architecture matter

Source: *An Introduction to Systems Biology: Design Principles of Biological Circuits*, 2007 title/copyright page checked, local 316-page PDF. The 162-page 8.591 scan had no embedded text in the first 12 pages; it was not substantively read. Used the readable 316-page edition instead.

Selected substantive reading: section 7.4.2-7.4.4, printed pp. 146-149 / PDF 163-166; exercise 7.3 and solution, printed pp. 155-156 / PDF 172-173. Contents and title pages were inspected to select this section. Extraction misrenders some printed page numerals; section and PDF indices are reliable locators.

SOURCE: total methylated receptor M = Xm+Xm* has dynamics

dM/dt = V_R R - V_B B A/(K+A),  A = Xm*.

The assumptions include saturated CheR reference flux and CheB acting on active receptors. At a feasible steady state,

A* = K V_R R / (V_B B - V_R R),  provided V_B B > V_R R.

The external ligand changes the fraction of receptors active, not the reference-flux expression. Consequently the equilibrium A* is independent of ligand under the model's assumptions. A* still depends on biochemical parameters; exact adaptation and a fixed universal setpoint are different properties.

SOURCE: the linearized exercise expresses the same mechanism as

dA/dt = a M - b A;     dM/dt = -K_I(A-A_target).

RECONSTRUCTION: at any finite equilibrium with K_I != 0, the second equation enforces A=A_target. Stability and actuator feasibility are additional requirements, not consequences of the equilibrium identity. Alon's text notes robustness failure outside saturation assumptions and approximate error when the substrate specificity assumption is relaxed.

Reasoning change: simply adding a homeostatic term to a neural circuit is not a mechanism for preserving computation. It must measure the right relation and have control authority. The candidate therefore exposes a learned trace critic as its central unresolved object rather than pretending that local activity homeostasis solves credit assignment.

Primary follow-up checked online: Yi, Huang, Simon and Doyle (2000), *Robust perfect adaptation in bacterial chemotaxis through integral feedback control*, PNAS 97, 4649-4653, DOI 10.1073/pnas.97.9.4649. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC18287/ . Results paragraph and Figure 2 distinguish stable integral feedback from arbitrary feedback. This reading confirms the stability qualification; no new theorem is claimed.

## 3. Ashby: constructing the regulator does not create information for free

Source: *An Introduction to Cybernetics*, local 306-page PDF. Complete extracted pages: printed 206-211 / PDF 216-221; printed 245-251 / PDF 255-261; printed 263-272 / PDF 273-282. Substantively read sections 11/5-11/11, 13/4-13/10, 13/22-13/23 and 14/1-14/6. Later extracted pages in the selection were not all inspected; extraction is not reading.

SOURCE, 11/5-11/7: under the table condition that no two entries in a column are equal, r disturbance rows and c response columns leave at least r/c distinct outcomes after one response is selected per row. With logarithmic variety, V_out >= V_disturbance - V_response. Section 11/9 explicitly changes this bound when columns themselves suppress variation. This is a conditional combinatorial result, not a universal assertion that more random neural variation helps.

SOURCE, 13/9-13/10: repeated disturbance/response sequences can be handled by a machine implementing the repeated response relation, rather than transmitting each individual response afresh. The book then asks how that regulatory machine itself is constructed.

SOURCE, 14/4-14/6: staged construction can amplify effective regulation by supplementation, including information arriving from the environment directly to the constructed regulator. The book's thermostat example charges a construction cost and compares it with the cost of repeated direct actions. It does not claim useful regulation appears from a low-information controller alone.

RECONSTRUCTION: if construction cost is C_build and runtime adaptation cost C_adapt, persistent construction becomes useful over N reuses only if C_build + N*C_run < N*C_direct (with the same accuracy). The task feedback, environment, library, and inherited construction process supply the missing information. This changes the candidate's cost model: critic training, teacher executions, edits and replay must be included; only the final circuit's standalone execution can justify amortization.

Reasoning change: a proposed local capture signal is a communication channel. If it cannot distinguish a correct stateful computation from an incorrect one, a generator cannot acquire the distinction by receiving more of the same signal. This directs the first adversarial check at information in the stabilization signal.
